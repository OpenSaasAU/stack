# Bare read and One hop become the ORM's, not ours

> **Amended 2026-09-02 by [Does One hop hold on both sides of a one-to-one now that .include() reaches the non-FK side?](https://github.com/OpenSaasAU/stack/issues/1097)** (Prisma 8 map). The one-to-one refusal below rested on an rc-era claim that the CLI gate ([#1070](https://github.com/OpenSaasAU/stack/issues/1070)) found false — `.include()` reaches a one-to-one from the non-FK side too — and is **dropped**, as the Consequences bullet here pre-committed. One hop holds on both sides; [ADR-0064](0064-one-hop-holds-on-both-sides-of-a-one-to-one-and-the-generator-emits-the-pair.md) records what the generator emits instead. The two withdrawals and the depth-cap re-homing are unaffected.

ADR-0024 and ADR-0026 hand-rolled two read-shape rules on top of Prisma 7: a read naming no relation returns the row's own columns, and naming a relation reaches exactly one hop. Prisma 8 enforces both natively — `.all()` returns own columns, `.include(rel)` stops at that relation — so we **withdraw both ADRs** rather than porting them. What they decided was "stop auto-including"; the ORM will not let that be re-fought, and an ADR whose decision can no longer be reversed is not a standing decision. The `CONTEXT.md` terms **Bare read** and **One hop** survive: they name guarantees the stack still makes to its callers, which is a different job from recording why we once had to build them.

Two things the withdrawn ADRs carried do not go with them, and are re-homed here.

**The depth cap keeps its number and loses its only stated reason.** `READ_INCLUDE_MAX_DEPTH` was justified by ADR-0026 as a cost limit over a tree the caller wrote, fail-closed per ADR-0022 — not as a guard against an engine walk that could explode across the relationship graph. That justification is the whole of why the number is a policy rather than an accident, and it survives the withdrawal: under ADR-0041's opaque wrapper a caller's nesting is `include(...)` refinements, countable at build time, so the cap is still enforceable and still refuses loudly. It is recorded here because the alternative — a constant in `depth-limits.ts` with no stated reason — is exactly the shape that gets "simplified" away by the next reader. ADR-0026's re-pointing of the **cycle guard** is not recorded here: it belongs with what it guards, and is absorbed into ADR-0025.

**One hop does not survive intact, and the break is in the generator.** _(Withdrawn by [ADR-0064](0064-one-hop-holds-on-both-sides-of-a-one-to-one-and-the-generator-emits-the-pair.md); the premise in the next sentence failed at `prisma@8.0.0-rc.12`.)_ Prisma 8's `.include()` reaches a one-to-one relation only from the FK-holding side, while the stack emits the non-FK side as an ordinary relation field (`packages/core/src/fields/index.ts`). One hop claims to hold for _any_ relation a caller names, and universality was the entire argument of both withdrawn ADRs — ADR-0024's case against the old default was that three read paths returned three shapes "differing by privilege rather than by anything the caller wrote", and ADR-0026 extended it on the grounds that "either the rule holds at every level or it is not a rule". So we **refuse the non-FK side of a one-to-one at generation** rather than let the rule carry an exception. The config already errors on FK-ownership conflicts one function away, so the seat exists and the error can name the field and the side that owns the key.

## Considered Options

- **Port the rules as ours anyway**, keeping the hand-rolled enforcement so the guarantee is ours to state: rejected. It is the auto-include walk kept alive to agree with the ORM, and every line of it is a line that can drift from what Prisma actually does.
- **Supersede ADR-0024 with a narrowed replacement** recording what remains ours — virtual fields on a bare read, and that the rule holds on the bypass path too: rejected. The virtual-field clause belongs to Field Visibility's definition, and the bypass clause is now _free_ rather than enforced, because `context.prisma` is raw Prisma 8 doing the same thing. Neither is a decision; both are descriptions, and descriptions live in `CONTEXT.md`.
- **Deleting the depth cap along with ADR-0026**, on the argument that a compiler-visible `include(...)` chain no longer needs a runtime budget: rejected. Visible is not bounded — a refinement callback can be generated — and the cap is a fail-closed backstop under ADR-0022, which is not the kind of thing to drop as a side effect of bookkeeping.
- **Accepting the one-to-one limit as a documented exception to One hop** _(the limit turned out not to exist — [ADR-0064](0064-one-hop-holds-on-both-sides-of-a-one-to-one-and-the-generator-emits-the-pair.md) — but the reasoning is kept, since the same option would recur if one ever did)_: rejected, as above. A relation field a caller cannot include is a field that lies about what it is, and the exception would have to be restated in every place One hop is.
- **Compensating in the wrapper** — issuing the inverse query when a caller includes from the non-FK side, so One hop holds uniformly: rejected. It preserves the guarantee by re-introducing exactly the hand-rolled read machinery this decision exists to delete.

## Consequences

- **ADR-0027 stands and is strengthened, not withdrawn.** It is not a projection ADR: it conditions our `resolveOutput` machinery on a projection, and Prisma knows nothing about computed fields. ADR-0041's "widen for the `needs` closure, then strip" is load-bearing on it — without the compute-iff-returned rule, widen-and-strip has no principle telling it what to compute. Its consequence scoping projection to the fragment path is amended there: ADR-0041 deleted the fragment API and made `.select()` universal, so selective computation now applies to every read.
- **The one-to-one refusal is a config-breaking change and rests on an rc-era claim.** It belongs on the map's GA re-verification gate alongside the other docs-versus-source items; if `.include()` reaches the non-FK side at GA, the refusal should be dropped rather than kept for tidiness. _Dropped: [#1070](https://github.com/OpenSaasAU/stack/issues/1070) measured it reaching, and [ADR-0064](0064-one-hop-holds-on-both-sides-of-a-one-to-one-and-the-generator-emits-the-pair.md) replaced the refusal with the `hasOne`/`belongsTo` emission._
- **The `CONTEXT.md` Projection entry's `_Avoid_` line inverts.** It warned against `select` as naming "an ORM feature this codebase does not honour"; ADR-0041 honours it exactly. `select` becomes the term's ORM spelling. The term itself is kept — it names the reciprocal-of-One-hop framing, the tie to ADR-0027, and ADR-0037's published-vocabulary clause, none of which `.select()` says on its own.
- **Nothing here settles implicit many-to-many.** `.include()` does not support it and Prisma 8 deletes the construct outright, but no Bare read or One hop guarantee mentions relation cardinality or junction shape, so neither rule breaks. The construct's fate is [#1047](https://github.com/OpenSaasAU/stack/issues/1047)'s.
- **The include path's internal materialisation of parent rows costs nothing today.** ADR-0041 materialises every terminal, and Field Visibility already runs over a materialised array. It is a live input to the streaming question rather than a problem now: a streaming secured read would be genuinely incremental only on include-free reads, so "can a secured read stream?" may not have a single answer.
