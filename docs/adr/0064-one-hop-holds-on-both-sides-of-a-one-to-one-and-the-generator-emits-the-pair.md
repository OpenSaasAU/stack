# One hop holds on both sides of a one-to-one, and the generator emits the pair

Status: accepted

[ADR-0043](0043-bare-read-and-one-hop-become-the-orms-not-ours.md) withdrew the hand-rolled read-shape rules and, on an rc-era reading that Prisma 8's `.include()` reaches a one-to-one only from the FK-holding side, refused the non-FK side at generation rather than let One hop carry an exception — and pre-committed to dropping that refusal if the claim failed at GA. [#1070](https://github.com/OpenSaasAU/stack/issues/1070) ran the claim against a CLI-emitted contract (`prisma@8.0.0-rc.12`, toolchain and runtime `8.0.0-rc.8`, PostgreSQL 14.20) and it failed: with `User.relations({ profile: rel.hasOne(Profile, { by: 'userId' }) })` opposite `Profile.relations({ user: rel.belongsTo(User, { from: 'userId', to: 'id' }) })`, `User.include('profile')` returns the profile, `null` where there is none, and honours a refinement `where` with the parent row kept. The refusal's premise is gone, so **the refusal goes with it: One hop holds on both sides of a one-to-one, with no generation error and no exception.** What ADR-0043 never had to decide — how the generator gets a `hasOne`/`belongsTo` pair out of one config declaration — is decided here.

## The pair is derived from the ownership rule the config already has

A one-to-one is declared as it is today: two `relationship()` fields, `many: false` on both, each `ref`-ing the other. Which side owns the foreign key is decided as it is today — `db.foreignKey: true` on one side, otherwise the alphabetically smaller list name, and for a self-referential pair the smaller field name; `true` on both sides stays a config error. Nothing new is asked of the config.

The generator then emits, from that one decision:

- **Owning side:** the FK column `<field>Id`, its `constraints.foreignKey(...)` to the target's id, a **unique constraint on that column**, and `rel.belongsTo(Target, { from: '<field>Id', to: 'id' })`.
- **Other side:** `rel.hasOne(Owner, { by: '<field>Id' })` — no column.

This is the derivation the to-many case already makes: the `many: false` side is `belongsTo`, the `many: true` side is `hasMany({ by })`. A one-to-one differs only in the inverse builder and in the unique constraint, and the builder's own shape says why the derivation is ours: `hasOne` takes the _same_ option as `hasMany` — the FK column on the other model — and the builder lowers it by `relation.kind` alone (`contract-builder-*.mjs:1080` at rc.8, `cardinality: kind === 'hasMany' ? '1:N' : '1:1'`). Prisma has no notion of which relation "is" one-to-one that we could delegate to.

**The unique constraint is the stack's to emit, and it is what makes the relation one-to-one.** Prisma's TypeScript builder validates a `hasOne` for field arity only (`lowerHasOwnershipRelation`); the one uniqueness check Prisma ships — `PSL_NON_UNIQUE_BACKRELATION` — lives in the PSL interpreter, on the path ADR-0040 does not use. The runtime decodes every to-one as `rows[0] ?? null` (`orm-client.mjs:2288`). So a `hasOne` over a non-unique column emits, applies, and silently returns _some_ row. The generator's unique constraint is the one thing between "one-to-one" and "first of many", which is the constraint-from-outside-the-file that earns a comment where the generator emits it. It is a port, not a new invariant: the Prisma 7 generator already puts `@unique` on the owning column.

## What this does and does not change

**Reads are symmetric; writes are not, and that is not an exception.** Both sides are to-one relation names a caller can `include`, filter with `some`/`every`/`none` (the Where vocabulary's accessor is built over every relation name and lowers each to an `EXISTS` whatever its cardinality — ADR-0055 stands unchanged), and receive under the same Access Filter and Field Visibility as any to-one: the related list's `query` access rides in as the refinement `where` (#1063), an outright `false` lowers to a never-matching predicate, and the value is `Row | null` by arity ([ADR-0058](0058-a-to-one-relation-reads-as-nullable-by-arity-not-by-column.md) named this exact case in advance). `connect` stays legal only on the owning side ([ADR-0050](0050-nested-relation-input-leaves-the-secured-write-surface.md)): reading through the inverse is a query the ORM issues against the other table; writing through it is a write to the other list's row, the hidden second write ADR-0050 exists to remove. ADR-0050 cited ADR-0043's refusal as precedent; the precedent is withdrawn and the rule stands on its own ground, which was always the one that mattered.

**The emitted contract's cardinality is asymmetric, and that is Prisma's spelling, not a bug to fix.** The `hasOne` side lowers to `'1:1'` and the `belongsTo` side to `'N:1'` — always; `belongsTo` has no one-to-one form. Both are to-one to the runtime (`isToOneCardinality`) and to ADR-0058's arity rule (`'1:1' | 'N:1'`). The stack's relationship graph stays config-derived ([ADR-0040](0040-the-generator-emits-a-typescript-contract-module-not-psl.md)) and knows the pair is one-to-one from the config; nothing reads one-to-one-ness back from the contract.

**Two config errors on the non-owning side survive, for the reason they already had: there is no column there.** `db.isNullable: false` on it stays refused (the FK column and its nullability live on the owning side), and a `db.indexes` entry naming it stays refused. Neither is a relation the caller cannot name; both are a column the config does not have.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the refusal for tidiness.** Rejected in advance by ADR-0043's own Consequences bullet, and the reason holds: a relation field a caller cannot include is a field that lies about what it is, and once the ORM reaches it there is nothing left for the refusal to protect.
- **Require `db.foreignKey` on every one-to-one**, making ownership explicit now that the inverse emits something. Rejected: the alphabetical default has decided ownership since Prisma 7 with the same error on a double claim, [ADR-0048](0048-the-deleted-psl-constructs-become-config-defaults-not-ddl.md) kept config defaults in exactly this shape, and the map's breaking-changes preference is not a reason to break something for no gain.
- **Emit the inverse as `hasMany` and take the first row in the wrapper.** Rejected: it re-introduces a hand-rolled read shape one hop down — the class ADR-0043 deleted — and types the side to-many where ADR-0058 types it `| null`.
- **Trust Prisma to enforce uniqueness of the `by` column.** Rejected on measurement: the TypeScript builder does not, and the runtime's `rows[0]` decode turns the omission into silently wrong data rather than an error.
- **Let a one-to-one be declared from one side only** (`ref: 'Profile'`, `many: false`, read as one-to-one). Rejected: a list-only `ref` already means many-to-one with a synthetic to-many back-relation, and a second meaning for the same spelling keyed on nothing visible at the call site is exactly the ambiguity One hop's "readable from the call site" clause forbids. A one-to-one is two declarations, as it is today.

## Consequences

- **ADR-0043 is amended in place**: its One hop paragraph, the two Considered Options that turned on the limit, and the Consequences bullet that scheduled this re-check. The withdrawals of ADR-0024/0026 and the depth-cap re-homing are untouched.
- **`CONTEXT.md`'s One hop entry loses its refusal clause.** The rule holds for every relation a caller can name, and there is no longer a relation the ORM cannot reach — the clause described a relation that does not exist.
- **ADR-0050 and ADR-0048 each cited the refusal as a precedent; both are amended in place.** ADR-0050's rule and ADR-0048's move each stand on their own stated grounds.
- **The consumer sweep shrinks by one entry and gains none.** No example config declares a one-to-one today — the only in-repo declarations are a TSDoc example on `db.foreignKey` and the docs pages — and one that did would generate. The build's generator work is the `hasOne` emission for the inverse and the unique constraint's port onto the contract builder.
- **One rider for the build, not a re-verification gate:** a self-referential one-to-one (`hasOne` and `belongsTo` on the same model) was not measured at rc.8. The builder shows no restriction on it, and `relationship-foreign-key.test.ts` already covers the ownership rule for that case.
- **ADR-0058's advance clause is now in force**: the inverse side is a to-one whose foreign key lives on the other table and is nullable in fact; it is typed `| null` with no special case.
